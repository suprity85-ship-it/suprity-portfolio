AI Product · Landing Page

finai tool + rupeezy mcp server

Product Designer

Rupeezy

Project overview

Rupeezy needed a dedicated home for FinAI, its AI-powered financial assistant, and for the Rupeezy MCP Server, which lets developers connect their own AI clients (Claude, VS Code, ChatGPT) to live Rupeezy data. I designed a single-scroll landing page that does three jobs at once: let anyone try FinAI immediately with zero signup friction, convert that free usage into a logged-in Rupeezy account, and give developers a clear, self-serve path to installing the MCP server.

The page is fully responsive, with a dark, AI-forward visual language that reads as more "product" and less "marketing" than Rupeezy's other landing pages.

Problem

Before this page, a curious visitor couldn't experience Rupeezy's AI without first creating an account, and there was no destination for developers interested in the MCP server. FinAI's value stayed invisible until after signup, the opposite of what an AI product needs to prove itself. The MCP server had no discoverable page, no install instructions and no way to track adoption.

Objectives

  • Increase FinAI's daily query volume among investors and the general public.
  • Convert anonymous visitors into logged-in Rupeezy accounts, measured by query-to-login conversion.
  • Drive MCP server installs among developers and AI builders.
  • Improve SEO for AI-finance search terms in India and position Rupeezy as an AI-first trading platform.

Target audience

Two audiences share one URL: investors who want quick, free AI answers about stocks, mutual funds, IPOs and F&O, and developers who want to wire Rupeezy's live market data into their own Claude, VS Code or ChatGPT setup. The hero and prompt sections speak to the first group and the MCP intro and installation guide speak to the second, so neither has to scroll past irrelevant content.

Design walkthrough

Navigation
A sticky bar keeps the Rupeezy wordmark, a "FinAI" pill, and the Login and Open Demat Account CTAs always reachable, so a visitor ready to convert never has to scroll back up.
Hero: the product itself
Under the H1 "FinAI – AI Powered Financial Assistant", a large input box is the actual product, not a screenshot of it. Four example prompt chips give one-tap starting points, and a "0/3 used" counter sets expectations about free queries from the first load.
MCP Server intro
A second H2, "Use FinAI or Build Your Own Financial AI", reframes the page from "try our assistant" to "or build your own". Six reassurance cards (100% Secured, Accurate Financial Data, Privacy First, Lightning Fast, AI Agent Integration, Simple Installation) do the trust work developers need before pasting a config into their own machine.
Installation guide
A three-tab switcher (Claude Desktop, VS Code, ChatGPT) walks through numbered steps: open settings, paste the Rupeezy MCP server URL, and authenticate to start querying live data like "Show my portfolio". A "Need more help?" banner catches anyone who gets stuck.
Capabilities
Six cards (AI Stock Analysis, News, IPO Intelligence, Market Trend Analysis, AI news feed, Personalized Queries) turn the abstract idea of "an AI assistant" into concrete things it can do.
Popular prompts
Twelve category-tagged prompt cards double as a discovery tool for undecided visitors and an SEO-friendly list of the exact questions FinAI answers.
Closing CTA and footer
"Start using FinAI for smarter investing" with Try FinAI and Open Demat Account side by side, backed by ISO, SEBI and zero-AMC trust markers and the full regulatory footer a SEBI-regulated broker needs.

Key interaction

The free-query flow drives conversion. The counter stays hidden at zero uses, becomes a visible reminder after the first and second queries, and opens a login modal about 0.9 seconds after the third answer, giving the user a moment to read before being asked to sign in. The modal leads with mobile OTP and pairs the ask with a soft upsell for a free demat account. Prompt chips and cards auto-submit, and the bottom "Try FinAI" button scrolls back up and focuses the input.

Design guidelines

Cards lift slightly on hover with a border-colour transition, buttons glow with a soft purple shadow, and the login modal fades and slides in rather than snapping open. The layout uses three breakpoints: three-column grids on desktop, two on tablet, and a single full-width column on mobile that keeps the full section order.

Success metrics

  • ≥15%query-to-login conversion
  • ≥30%page views clicking a prompt
  • ≥5%MCP install click rate
  • ≥8%account-opening clicks
  • ≥2.0queries per session
  • ≤40%bounce rate

goal based investment calculator

Overview

The calculator lets a visitor pick a life goal (Home, Vacation, Gadgets, Car, Education, Wedding or a custom goal), set a target amount and timeline, and instantly see a personalised monthly SIP plan, a projected growth chart and a recommended fund basket. It works entirely without login, which makes it a strong top-of-funnel tool. A few of its most valuable insights sit behind a free OTP sign-in, turning a dead-end utility into an active lead-generation page.

Problem & opportunity

Investors think in goals, like "₹50L for a home in 5 years", but most SIP calculators only return an abstract monthly number with no path, projection or fund guidance. This kind of page already ranks well and attracts intent-rich traffic, yet gave visitors no reason to register once they had their answer. The opportunity was to keep the calculator genuinely useful and free at its core, and put a light, curiosity-driven gate on the few insights valuable enough to justify a sign-in.

Who it's for

  • First-time planners who want an honest "how much per month" number.
  • Comparison shoppers from search, weighing paths and projected returns.
  • Ready-to-invest users who want the exact funds. They are the prime sign-up target.

Design walkthrough

Trust and framing
"SEBI & AMFI Registered | ISO 27001 Certified" sits above the calculator, along with a promo bar for free demat account opening, so the tool carries the brand's credibility.
Goal switcher
Pill tabs let a visitor change goals inline without losing their place. The Custom tab shows a lock icon before it's clicked, an honest signal rather than a surprise gate.
Configure panel
A large goal-amount field with quick-add chips, a completion-time selector with a plain-language confirmation ("Your goal will meet the target in 5 years"), and an optional lumpsum field. Results recompute instantly as inputs change.
Suggested SIP paths
Four paths sit side by side (Safe Path, Steady Move, Growth Booster, Reach Faster), with the selected one summarised as Monthly SIP, Estimated Corpus, Total Investment and Estimated Returns. This turns an abstract goal into a concrete monthly number across four risk appetites.
Growth projection
A stacked area chart plots invested amount against projected corpus year by year, giving visual weight to the idea that disciplined investing compounds.
Suggested basket
A named basket card sits under the chart with its fund detail behind a soft lock and an "Unlock for free" CTA, right where a convinced visitor's attention lands.
Compliance and SEO
A clear market-risk disclaimer sits directly under the results. A collapsed overview accordion holds long-form SEO copy, and a five-question FAQ answers the objections most likely to stall a sign-up.

The lock mechanic

Locked content uses a soft blur rather than a hard block, so the value of what's hidden stays visible, paired with a small overlay: one headline, one line of subtext and a single "Unlock free" CTA. Only three elements are gated: the Growth Booster path's returns, the basket's fund detail and the Custom goal flow. Signing in through the mobile OTP modal unlocks every gate for the session at once.

Success metrics (first 90 days)

  • ≥55%sessions changing an input
  • ≥40%engaged sessions seeing a gate
  • ≥18%unlock click-through
  • ≥25%sign-in conversion

Outcome

The calculator makes a goal feel achievable in concrete monthly terms while quietly building a lead funnel around the moment a visitor is most convinced: right after they've seen their own growth chart and want the underlying funds. It stays free and indexable at its core, with just three low-friction gates.

Overview

Rupeezy wanted one clear, trustworthy page where anyone thinking about becoming a Mutual Fund Distributor could get their two biggest questions answered fast: "Can I trust this platform?" and "How much can I actually earn?" The page was built to answer exactly that, and to turn that trust into sign-ups.

What we were solving for

Becoming an MFD is a livelihood decision, not a casual click. The page had to explain a fairly technical business model (commissions, AUM, trail income, empanelment) in a way that feels honest and easy to follow, for someone starting from zero and for an experienced ARN holder comparing Rupeezy against the platform they already use. The main goal was qualified sign-ups and leads; building brand trust was the secondary win.

Who the page talks to

  • New distributors who want to know whether this is worth starting.
  • Existing ARN holders who distribute elsewhere and are checking whether Rupeezy's terms are better.

The language and proof points had to land for both: not too basic for the experienced person, not too jargon-heavy for the newcomer.

How success is measured

  • Leadsgenerated by the page
  • CTRon the lead form
  • SEOranking and organic traffic for industry terms

Mobile App · Redesign

rupeezy app home screen redesign

Role
Product Designer
Company
Rupeezy
Platform
iOS & Android
Themes
Dark and light

Project overview

Rupeezy's home/explore screen was rebuilt from a mutual-fund-led investing experience into a full trading dashboard. The old screen pitched mutual funds first and trading tools second; the redesign flips that order, leading with live portfolio value, market data and trading tools while keeping mutual funds, baskets and SIPs one tab away. It covers dark and light themes, plus both a funded user's home and a zero-balance "get started" state.

Problem

The old screen was built around getting users into a first mutual fund: a "Ready to Invest?" banner, hand-picked fund carousels, SIP tracking, calculators and an "Expert Help" call flow. That worked for a first-time investor but left active traders with no portfolio snapshot, no market scanners, no F&O tools and no stock news on their home screen. The most valuable screen in the app served one persona at the expense of another.

Objectives

  • Put portfolio value and market activity front and centre for returning traders.
  • Surface trading tools (F&O scanner, scalper, margin trading, option strategy builder) without digging.
  • Keep mutual funds and baskets reachable without letting them dominate.
  • Give new, unfunded users a clear path to their first deposit.
  • Work across both dark and light themes.

What changed

Header and navigation
A generic greeting and search icon became a segmented tab bar (Stocks, F&O, Mutual Funds, Commodity) with a live NIFTY50 and SENSEX ticker, signalling "this is a trading app" from the first glance.
Portfolio snapshot first
The screen now opens with Current Value, Today's Profit, Unrealised P&L and Invested amount, with a last-refreshed timestamp. For a zero-balance user, the same slot shows ₹0.00 with a single "Add Funds to start" CTA.
Trading tools surfaced early
Products (MTF Stocks, ETFs, Pledge Stocks, 5X T+5, IPOs, Commodities) and Tools get their own rows near the top, followed by a Quick Access grid (FO Scanner, Scalper Pro, 5X T+5 margin, Option Strategy) one tap away.
Curated content around expertise
Fund carousels became "Recommended for You – By SEBI Experts" stock and F&O calls with expected profit and days left, plus Curated Baskets with minimum amount, 3-year return and volatility.
Market data over banners
Illustration-heavy banners were replaced with a Nifty 50 list with gainer/loser filters, Key Business Groups, and a Trending News feed with sentiment tags and linked tickers.
Trust, restyled
Regulatory badges and social proof carry over in a simpler "Trade with confidence. Zero regrets." strip with SEBI, exchange and ISO badges.

Design guidelines

Both themes share one information architecture; the light theme adds a live market-status banner in place of the dark theme's "what's new" banner. Real-time numbers (P&L, prices, % change) take priority over illustration, and the same card patterns repeat across products, tools, recommendations and news so the screen reads as one system.

Outcome

The redesign repositions Rupeezy from "an app for investing in mutual funds that also lets you trade" to "a trading platform that also gives you curated investing options", while the zero-balance state keeps the simple, guided feel new users had before.

Website Design · Promo Landing Page

rupeezy web promo landing page

Role
Product Designer
Company
Rupeezy
Platform

Project overview

Rupeezy had no dedicated page promoting its WebApp. Most users associated the brand with its mobile app, and campaigns had nowhere purpose-built to send traffic. I designed a desktop-first promo page to become the main destination for Rupeezy Web: one page that introduces the platform, shows its trading and charting capabilities, builds trust through a founder message and reviews, and converts visitors into demat account holders.

Problem

Rupeezy Web had no visibility of its own. Users thought of Rupeezy as mobile-first, campaigns had no landing page, and the WebApp's real capabilities (real-time charting, instant execution, portfolio tools) went largely unseen. Getting mobile users onto the web had no natural entry point.

Objectives

  • Primary: grow visibility and usage of Rupeezy Web, strengthen brand positioning as a serious trading platform, drive demat account openings and grow cross-platform adoption.
  • Secondary: increase session duration and engagement, support SEO and paid campaigns, and educate visitors on what the platform can do.

Target audience

Active, F&O and intraday traders, plus existing Rupeezy users who may not know the web platform exists, with a secondary audience of new and long-term investors and people comparing brokers. That shaped the balance between advanced features (option chains, scalping, 100+ indicators) and simpler trust-building content (founder message, reviews, a three-step account opening flow).

Design walkthrough

Hero
A "SEBI & AMFI Registered | ISO 27001 Certified" strip sits above the headline "Trade smarter on Rupeezy Web". Watch Demo and Access Web App CTAs lead into a large laptop mockup of the live dashboard, so the product makes the first impression.
Key features
Six cards (Real-Time Charts, Portfolio Insights, Instant Execution, Scalper, AI News Feed, Bank-Grade Security) with the line "From your first SIP to advanced F&O, Rupeezy supports every step", speaking to both first-time investors and experienced traders.
WebApp previews
A tab bar (Dashboard, FinAI, Markets, Orders, Watchlist, Portfolio, Expert Picks) swaps full-width screenshots of each module. This is the page's proof section.
Advanced charts
100+ indicators, multiple chart types, drawing tools and historical data sit beside a live candlestick chart, showing charting as a first-class part of the product.
Philosophy and founder message
Stat badges (5L downloads, 20+ years of trust, ISO 27001) and a founder note from CEO Shauryam Gupta put a named, credentialed person behind the brand.
Reviews
A 4.6/5 rating from 14K reviews above a scrollable set of testimonial cards gives social proof from real users.
Get started and FAQ
A three-step flow (create account, add funds, start investing) leads into a single Open Demat Account CTA, and a five-question FAQ handles the objections most likely to stall a conversion.

Design guidelines

A premium trading-terminal aesthetic with Rupeezy branding, smooth scroll-triggered interactions, high-fidelity dashboard screenshots in place of illustration, and subtle hover treatment on cards and CTAs. A sticky desktop CTA keeps "Access Web App" reachable across all nine sections.

Success metrics

  • CTRWebApp access clicks
  • Conv.account opening rate
  • >2 minsession duration target
  • Growthin WebApp users

Graphic Design · App Store Optimization

app store creatives

Role
Designer
Company
Rupeezy
Channels
Play Store, App Store

The problem

Store screenshots that show raw app screens with no context make a browsing user guess what they're looking at, or install the app just to find out.

The approach

Each card pairs a real screen from the app with a single, benefit-led headline:

  • Zero account opening and AMC charges
  • FinAI as a financial assistant
  • Stock and ETF investing with SIP, GTT and price protection
  • 5X margin funding
  • An intuitive F&O option chain
  • SIPs from ₹100
  • Easy MCX commodity trading
  • Online IPO applications

Why it matters

Visitors come to a listing for different reasons. A trader wants margin and F&O, an investor wants SIPs and IPOs, and these cards let each spot "this app does what I need" in a two-second scroll. They also show the full breadth of Rupeezy (AI tools, commodities, IPOs, not just trading), which should help both listing-to-install conversion and the quality of who installs, since expectations are set correctly upfront.
